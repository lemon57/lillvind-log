Arseny had planned a family outing to Dragør, a charming Danish harbour town just south of Copenhagen, and invited me along. It looked like a fine trip, though I couldn't return on the boat — I needed to be home earlier — so I decided to make it one-way: sail over with the crew and take the train back.

I arrived at the marina around 9:00 AM to find the boat already prepared and ready to go. We motored out of the harbour, raised the sails, and got underway. The wind was light, but the current worked in our favour and we made steady progress toward the Øresund Bridge at around 4 knots. We passed under it on engine, set a course for Dragør, and watched the wind gradually fade. Once we crossed the shipping lane, it died completely.

We started the engine and motored the rest of the way — the right call. The sea was nearly glassy. Arseny made the most of the calm by trying his luck at trolling from the cockpit; he looked every bit the seasoned angler, but not a single bite came.

On approach to Dragør we dropped the sails and came in under power. We turned into the left wing of the harbour, where free berths were few, but we found one. On the way to the spot the engine briefly stalled — struggling to hold idle — but it restarted without fuss and we arrived without further trouble. We had to use longer mooring lines, though in the end we used the ones already rigged in the berth, with lines ready on the dock posts at the bow.

Walking around the harbour afterward, we noticed a different type of berth we hadn't seen before: bow-in, with a stern line picked up from the bottom. These spots sat closer to the tourist area, right next to the harbour fee station — probably the more practical choice if you're only stopping for lunch, since paying and leaving is quicker and the bow-in approach is easy.

Dragør turned out to be a genuinely lovely and touristic little town. Unfortunately I had to leave before seeing any of it. No matter — we'll make it back there again. I said goodbye to Arseny and his family crew and headed for the train. The journey home took just over an hour.

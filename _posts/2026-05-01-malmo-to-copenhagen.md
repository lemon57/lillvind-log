We had wanted for a long time to cross the strait and tie up in a Danish harbour — a short but still international trip under our own keel. Another idea was to sail to work by yacht. May 1st turned out to be perfect for it. In Sweden it's a public holiday, Labour Day, while in Denmark it's just a normal working day.

We chose a medium-distance route: not too short, not too long, and with a harbour that had easy transport access back to the city. The choice fell on Sundby Sejlforening guest harbour.

![Route map — Malmö to Copenhagen Sundby harbour]({{ '/assets/images/trip-swe-kph-route-map-2026-05-01.jpg' | relative_url }})
*The route: out from Malmö, past Peberholm, under the bridge, south to Sundby harbour.*

## Departure

Roman joined me for the crossing to Denmark. We were up before sunrise. By 5 a.m. I was already aboard, warming up the engine and preparing the boat for departure. Together we rigged the jib, and at 6 a.m., with the first sunlight, we left the harbour.

![Morning departure, glassy water on the Øresund]({{ '/assets/images/trip-swe-kph-morning-departure-2026-05-01.jpg' | relative_url }})
*First light on the Øresund — not a breath of wind.*

## Glass-Flat Sea, Engine All the Way

The day was sunny, warm, and completely calm. There was no chance for true sailing, but it still felt wrong to waste such a good day staying ashore. The crossing itself was easy and uneventful. The engine behaved well, we put the autopilot on for most of the route, and simply watched the coast slide by.

We passed large ships, fishing boats, and all kinds of seabirds. One of the most interesting parts was passing close to the airport and watching airliners descend overhead on approach. Closer to the harbour we even passed a sunken ship.

![Øresund Bridge from the water]({{ '/assets/images/trip-swe-kph-oresund-bridge-2026-05-01.jpg' | relative_url }})
*Passing under the bridge — one of those views that never gets old.*

## Coming Into Sundby

Entering the harbour was straightforward, though slightly tense because the water gets shallow on the approach. As usual in an unfamiliar harbour, we managed to take the wrong turn first. The berths on that side were too long for our boat, so once again we had to use our long stern lines. Good thing we carry them.

The crossing took around three and a half hours on engine.

![Approaching Sundby harbour, wind farm visible]({{ '/assets/images/trip-swe-kph-sundby-arrival-2026-05-01.jpg' | relative_url }})
*Almost there — wind farm and shallow water ahead.*

After paying the harbour fee at the machine — 50 DKK, with a discount thanks to the "Free Port" sticker — Ivan managed to cut his leg on a low water tap sticking out near the ground. Not the most elegant place to install a water tap. Nothing serious though, it'll heal.

## The Return

After work we met again around 16:30. The engine started immediately without even needing a warm-up. This time the crew was three: Ivan, Roma, and Hans — an AI specialist from Boozt.

The weather stayed good, around +20°C, but still no wind. We motored back home under autopilot, enjoying the scenery. This time we passed even closer to the airport and planes flew directly overhead — quite a sight.

Near the Øresund Bridge we encountered the Swedish Visby-class corvette. It moved incredibly fast and left heavy waves behind it.

We reached our home harbour around 19:00, a little quicker than the outbound trip. Three engine hours. We folded the sails, packed the mainsail back into the lazy bag, and headed home tired but satisfied.

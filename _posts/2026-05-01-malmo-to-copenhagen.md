May 1st. On the boat at 5:30, cast off at 6:30. Quiet harbour, empty water. A clean start.

## Glass-Flat Sea, Engine All the Way

The forecast said northwest wind up to 13 km/h. In practice — nothing. Flat calm. Not a ripple. Not sailing weather, but honestly? Perfect for everything else. The air was around +12°C when we set off and climbed toward +20°C by midday. Sunshine, no clouds, warm on the face.

The water was so still you could see straight to the bottom — sand, rocks, patches of weed. Transparent. We ran the engine the whole way.

![Morning departure, glassy water on the Øresund]({{ '/assets/images/trip-swe-kph-morning-departure-2026-05-01.jpg' | relative_url }})
*First light on the Øresund — not a breath of wind.*

## Under the Bridge and Beyond

We crossed two shipping lanes — both times with big ships and tankers moving through. You wait for your gap, cross fast, and get out of the way.

Around Peberholm island we had to swing further toward the bridge than feels natural — shallow water forces you wide — before turning south toward Copenhagen.

Past the bridge the wind farms start. The big one sits just behind the bridge; a smaller cluster right in front of Sundby harbour. Somewhere near that second farm we spotted a sunk boat, mast leaning, half below the waterline. Still sitting there. You can see it in the gallery.

![Øresund Bridge from the water]({{ '/assets/images/trip-swe-kph-oresund-bridge-2026-05-01.jpg' | relative_url }})
*Passing under the bridge — one of those views that never gets old.*

## Coming Into Sundby

The final stretch into Sundby requires patience. Depth drops to 2 metres or less. You follow the marked channel and go slow. We took it carefully, found a berth, got her tied up.

![Approaching Sundby harbour, wind farm visible]({{ '/assets/images/trip-swe-kph-sundby-arrival-2026-05-01.jpg' | relative_url }})
*Almost there — wind farm and shallow water ahead.*

## The Return

The trip back was just as good. More light, better angles — planes, birds, the bridge from the other side. Some of those shots are in the gallery.

One way: roughly 3.5 hours. Well spent.

If you like boats, the sea, or just being outside on a good day — this crossing is worth doing. Highly recommended.

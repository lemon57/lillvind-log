On June 6th we took the children out on the water for the first time. A calm, steady breeze was blowing, and the day felt made for it.

We decided to head toward the Øresund Bridge — a destination that's always impressive, and one that tends to capture a child's imagination straight away. The kids loved every minute. They took turns with the binoculars, watched the bridge grow larger as we approached, and peered up at it from close range. Between the sightseeing they kept a close eye on Roman and me as we set the sails and trimmed them, curious about what we were doing and why. There was also tea and buns, which went down very well.

We edged close enough to the bridge to see the trains passing overhead clearly, and the kids noticed the height markers on the pillars — 40 m and 54 m — and started asking what they meant. We explained that the signs mark the clearance height: how much room there is for tall ships to pass underneath.

From where we were I had my first clear view of two large ferries meeting and crossing each other directly under the bridge — quite a sight. We then sailed along the bridge span, crossed the main shipping channel, passed under the central arch, and turned back toward home.

On the return leg the children started to wind down. They disappeared below to explore the cabin and took turns poking their heads out of the forward hatch to watch the water rush past.

All told we covered around 15 kilometres and the whole trip took about two to two and a half hours. A short sail by any measure, but the kids came away buzzing with questions and full of impressions.
